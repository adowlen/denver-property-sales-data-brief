# LinkedIn post draft - Denver property sales brief (for Alex's review)

$595,000.

That was Denver's median market sale price at its 2022 peak. It was $450,000 in 2020. In 2025 it was $525,000 - and it has sat in a $525,000-$550,000 band for three straight years.

We read the whole ledger this time: 105,585 market sales in the Denver assessor's sales and transfers file, 2020 through the October 15, 2026 snapshot (2026 partial), joined to the permit and demolition records by parcel.

The bottom of the market is the story inside the story. In 2020, 57.2% of market sales closed at or under $500,000. In 2022: 38.6%. In 2025 it recovered to 47.4% - partway back, not all the way.

By neighborhood, the median single-family sale runs from $335,000 in Globeville to $2,195,000 in Cherry Creek. And the teardown cycle now has prices on both ends: across the 611 parcels that completed a recorded purchase-demolition-permit-sale cycle inside the record, the median purchase was $650,000 and the median first sale after the rebuild permit was $2,299,525 - the median difference between the two deeds is $1,635,000, before construction and financing costs.

One honest caveat, printed in the brief: these are nominal dollars, and medians describe what sold, not a price index. The mix of homes that sell moves the median on its own.

Full brief, charts, and reproducible code: https://github.com/adowlen/denver-property-sales-data-brief

Free for advocates and journalists, as always. The data was already public. It just needed to be read as one ledger instead of 313,361 separate transfers.

# X thread draft - Denver property sales brief (for Alex's review; he posts manually; no hashtag line)

1/ We read Denver's entire sales ledger: 105,585 market sales, 2020-2026 (2026 partial), from the assessor's transfers file. The median price: $450,000 in 2020, a peak of $595,000 in 2022, then $525,000-$550,000 for three straight years. Volume peaked in 2021 and fell 45% by 2023.

2/ The bottom of the market thinned out and only partly refilled. Share of sales at or under $500,000: 57.2% in 2020, 38.6% in 2022, 47.4% in 2025. Sales under $250,000 went from 19.7% of the market to 11.8% and back to 21.2%.

3/ Neighborhood single-family medians, pooled 2020-2026 (2026 partial): Cherry Creek $2,195,000. Country Club $1,625,000. Globeville $335,000. Elyria Swansea $336,663. The top of the city medians 6.6 times the bottom.

4/ Between 2020-21 and 2024-25 the citywide single-family median rose 11.2%. Southmoor Park +35.6% (on 88 and 65 sales), Belcaro +31.2%, Cole +27.0%. Four neighborhoods went down: Lincoln Park -10.4%, University Park -9.8%, Cheesman Park -7.5%, Five Points -6.3%. Nominal dollars, and medians track what sold - both caveats are in the brief.

5/ The teardown cycle, priced: 3,297 parcels carry a demolition permit dated since 2020; 70.8% later received a new-building permit on the parcel record (a floor - recent demolitions have had less time). The 611 parcels that completed the recorded cycle: median buy $650,000, median first sale after the rebuild permit $2,299,525, median difference between the deeds $1,635,000 over 28.3 months - before construction and financing costs. Not a profit figure.

6/ Full brief, charts, and reproducible code: https://github.com/adowlen/denver-property-sales-data-brief

Every figure traces to Denver's own assessor and permit records, snapshot of October 15, 2026, with an independent verification pass in the repo.
